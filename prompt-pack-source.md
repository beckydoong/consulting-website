## SET THIS UP FIRST — Your Voice (5 minutes)
Paste this at the top of a Custom GPT (ChatGPT) or a Project (Claude). Every prompt that says "in my voice" pulls from it, so your output sounds like you, not like everyone else's AI.

```
I am [1 line: who you are + the skill you sell].
I help [who] do [what] so they can [outcome].
Voice — 5 rules: [...]; always: [...]; never: [...].
Proof: [2–3 real wins, results, or things people say about your work].
```
Two rules for every prompt: swap the [brackets], and always take the second draft. Each prompt is tagged for where it runs best — ChatGPT (fast, punchy), Claude (long context, nuance), or Either.

## STAGE 1 — FIND YOUR EDGE
*The skill people will actually pay you for. Seven prompts.*

**01. The Skills Inventory** · Either · *When: you don't know what you'd even sell.*
"List everything I've gotten good at, paid or not, from this: [jobs, hobbies, things people ask me for help with]. Sort into three buckets: skills people pay for, skills people pay a premium for, and skills that are too common to charge for. For each paid one, name who buys it and a rough market rate. Flag the 3 with the best mix of real demand and work I'd actually enjoy."

**02. People Already Ask Me This** · Either · *When: your edge is hiding in plain sight.*
"Here's what friends, coworkers, and family come to me for help with: [paste]. Which of these is a real, payable service? For each, name the person who'd pay for it, the problem it solves, and what they'd type into Google to find it. Rank by how quickly someone could start earning from it."

**03. The Hidden Expertise Finder** · Claude · *When: you think you 'don't have a skill.'*
"Interview me to surface a sellable skill. Ask one question at a time about my work history, the problems I've solved, and what comes easy to me that others find hard. After 6 questions, name my 3 strongest income angles and point to the evidence in my own answers."

**04. The Demand Check** · Either · *When: before you bet on a skill.*
"I'm considering selling [skill]. Show me the real demand: who already pays for this, roughly what they pay, where they look for it, and 3 signs the market is growing or shrinking. If demand looks thin, say so plainly and suggest a stronger adjacent skill I already have."

**05. The Skill-to-Income Matcher** · Either
"Given my skills [paste] and the time I have per week [X hours], match each skill to its fastest path to income: freelance service, digital product, done-with-you, or paid content. For each, give the first offer to sell, the realistic time to first dollar, and the income ceiling."

**06. The Unfair Advantage** · Claude
"Based on my background [paste], what can I offer that most people selling [skill] can't? Name my unfair advantage in one sentence, who values it most, and the proof I'd use to back it. No generic 'passion' answers — tie it to something specific in my history."

**07. The Pick-One Decision** · Either · *When: too many ideas, no focus.*
"Here are the income ideas I'm weighing: [paste]. Score each 1–5 on demand, speed to first dollar, how much I'd enjoy it, and fit with my skills. Show the scoring, name the winner, and tell me the one to start this month and why the rest can wait."

## STAGE 2 — SHAPE THE OFFER
*Turn a skill into one clear thing people can buy. Seven prompts.*

**08. The Productizer** · Claude
"Turn my skill [skill] into one clear offer. Define the exact outcome the buyer gets, who it's for, what's included, what's not, and how it's delivered. Write it as a one-paragraph offer a stranger understands in 10 seconds. Avoid vague words like 'strategy' or 'support' — name the actual deliverable."

**09. Service, Product, or Done-With-You?** · Either
"I can sell [skill] as a service (done for them), a product (they do it themselves), or done-with-you. For my situation [paste], compare all three on time-per-sale, price ceiling, and how fast I could launch. Recommend the one to start with and the order to add the others."

**10. The Transformation Definer** · Either
"Define the before-and-after my offer creates. Before: where the client is stuck, in their words. After: the specific result, with a number or proof if possible. Then the one-sentence promise that bridges the two. No hype, nothing I can't actually deliver."

**11. The Smallest Sellable Version** · Claude · *When: you're overbuilding.*
"Here's the offer I'm planning: [paste]. Strip it to the smallest version someone would still pay for today. Tell me what to cut, what to keep, and the price for the stripped version. The goal is a first sale this month, not a perfect product."

**12. The Offer Namer** · Either
"Give me 10 names for this offer: [paste]. Mix plain-and-clear with one or two that have a hook. Each should tell the buyer what they get. No clever-but-confusing. Flag the 3 clearest and the 1 with the most pull."

**13. The Offer One-Liner** · Either
"Write 5 versions of 'I help [who] do [what] so they can [outcome]' for my offer: [paste]. Each specific enough that the right person reads it and thinks 'that's me.' Vary the angle. Flag the strongest and say why."

**14. The Offer Stress-Test** · Claude · *When: before you launch it. (recast)*
"Here's my offer and price: [paste]. Play the skeptical buyer in my market. List the 7 objections that actually stop the sale, ranked by how often they'd come up, and the single strongest, plainspoken answer to each. No strawmen I can knock down easily."

## STAGE 3 — PRICE IT
*What to charge, and how to say it without flinching. Seven prompts.*

**15. The Pricing Model Picker** · Either
"For my offer [paste], compare pricing models: hourly, per-project, tiered packages, retainer, or a flat product price. Show the trade-offs for someone just starting out, and recommend one with a starting number based on the outcome I deliver, not the hours I spend."

**16. The Value-Based Price** · Claude
"Help me price on value, not hours. The result my offer creates is [paste outcome]. Walk through what that result is worth to the buyer, then back into a price that's a clear yes for them and a real win for me. Give a low / target / stretch number with the reasoning behind each."

**17. The Package Builder** · Either
"Turn my single offer [paste] into 3 tiers: good, better, best. For each, give the name, what's included, who it's for, and the price. Make the middle tier the obvious pick. Keep the differences real, not padded."

**18. The Price Anchor** · Either
"I want to sell [offer] at [price]. Give me 3 honest ways to make that price feel like an easy yes: what to compare it against, what to include that costs me little but means a lot, and how to present it. No fake urgency, no fake discounts."

**19. 'What Do You Charge?' Script** · Either · *When: the price question makes you freeze.*
"Write exactly what I say when a prospect asks my price for [offer] at [price]. Confident, no apologizing, no over-explaining. Give me the line, then how to handle 'that's more than I expected' without dropping the price."

**20. From Free to Paid** · Claude · *When: you've been doing it for free.*
"I've been doing [skill] for free for [who]. Write the message that moves them to paying — honest, warm, no guilt. Name the value they've already gotten, what changes now, and the price. Give me one version for a friend and one for a referral."

**21. The Raise-Your-Rate Plan** · Either
"I currently charge [X] for [offer]. Give me a 3-step plan to reach [target] over [timeframe]: who to raise first, what to say, and what to add so the higher price is obviously fair. Flag the risk at each step."

## STAGE 4 — FIND YOUR FIRST CLIENTS
*Where they are, and how to reach out without the cringe. Seven prompts.*

**22. Who Exactly Pays You** · Either · *(recast)*
"Here's who I think buys my offer: [paste]. Tear it apart — where is it too broad? Rewrite it 3 ways, each narrowed to one real person: their situation, the moment they go looking for help, and the words they'd type. No 'anyone who needs [skill].'"

**23. The Angle Finder** · Claude · *(recast)*
"Here's my offer: [paste]. Give me 5 positioning angles that others selling this aren't using. For each: the wedge in one sentence, who it wins, who it loses, and the one line of copy that lands it. Rank by hardest to copy."

**24. Where They Gather** · Either
"My buyer is [paste]. List the 10 specific places they actually spend time — subreddits, Facebook groups, events, newsletters, hashtags, local spots. For each, how I'd show up useful instead of spammy, and the first action to take this week."

**25. The Warm List** · Either
"Walk me through building a list of 25 people I already know who could buy, refer, or amplify my offer [paste]. Give me the categories to scan — past coworkers, old clients, classmates, neighbors — and a one-line, non-awkward opener for each category."

**26. The First-Client Message** · Claude · *When: reaching out warm or cold.*
"Write a short outreach message to [person or type] for my offer [paste]. Lead with them, not me. Name a specific reason I'm reaching out, make one clear ask, and skip the pitch-slap. Give me a warm version and a cold version, each under 90 words."

**27. Mine Your Network** · Either · *(recast)*
"From my replies, DMs, and comments: [paste], find the 5 people showing a real need my offer solves. For each: quote the signal, write the opening line in my voice, and name the offer angle that fits. Skip the polite-but-cold ones."

**28. The Referral Ask** · Either
"Write the message I send to a happy client or contact to ask for a referral to my offer [paste]. Make it easy to say yes and easy to forward: who I'm looking for in one line, plus a copy-paste intro they can send on. No begging, no over-thanking."

## STAGE 5 — SELL WHAT YOU KNOW
*The words that turn interest into a paying client. Eight prompts.*

**29. The Hook Generator** · Either · *(recast)*
"In my voice, give me 12 hooks on [topic tied to my offer] for [buyer]. Mix contrarian, stat, story, and question — label each by type. Each names a concrete tension, number, or moment; no vague promises, no clickbait; under 12 words. Flag your top 3 and why."

**30. The Pitch in 5 Lines** · Either
"Write my offer pitch in 5 lines for [buyer]: the problem, the result, what's included, the price, the next step. Plainspoken and specific, no hype. Then give me a one-line version for a bio or a DM."

**31. The Sales Page Hero** · Claude · *(recast)*
"In my voice, write 5 hero blocks (headline + subhead + button) for my offer [paste], aimed at [buyer]. Vary claim / story / problem / comparison / contrarian. Lead with a specific outcome or number — clarity over cleverness. Distinct calls to action."

**32. The Proof-Point Rewriter** · Either · *(recast)*
"In my voice, rewrite each of these claims as a concrete proof point a skeptic would believe — add a number, a mechanism, or an example. Never invent figures: leave a [fill: ___] where I need to add real data. Flag any claim too vague to prove. Claims: [paste]."

**33. The One-Pager** · Either · *(recast)*
"Draft the copy and layout for a one-page sell sheet for my offer [paste], for [buyer], in my voice: headline, the problem, the solution, 3 benefits (each tied to a concrete outcome, not adjectives), proof, and a call to action. Tight, scannable, clear hierarchy."

**34. The Discovery-Call Script** · Claude
"Write a simple structure for a 20-minute call to close [offer]: the 5 questions that surface their problem, how to present my offer as the fix, how to handle the price moment, and how to end with a clear next step. Give me the actual words for the awkward parts."

**35. The Proposal** · Claude
"Draft a one-page proposal for [client] for [offer] at [price]: their problem in their words, what I'll deliver, the timeline, the price, and how to say yes. Confident and clear, no legalese, fits on one screen."

**36. The Repurpose Engine** · Either · *(recast)*
"In my voice, repurpose this win or post: [paste] into 3 LinkedIn posts, 1 short thread, 3 Reels hooks, and 1 outreach message. Pull the strongest ideas verbatim — don't paraphrase or invent stats. Each leads with a different angle."

## STAGE 6 — DELIVER LIKE A PRO
*Look organized, protect your time, get the testimonial. Seven prompts.*

**37. The Kickoff Questions** · Either
"Give me the 8 questions to ask a new client at kickoff for [offer] so I get everything I need up front and look organized. Group them, and say in one line why each matters."

**38. The Scope Liner** · Claude
"Write a plain-language scope for [offer] for [client]: what's included, what's not, the deliverables, the timeline, and what I need from them. Prevents scope creep without sounding rigid. One page."

**39. The Delivery Plan** · Either
"Turn [offer] into a simple delivery plan: the phases, what the client sees at each one, and the checkpoints. Keep it to something I can actually run solo."

**40. The Client Welcome** · Either · *(recast)*
"Write the welcome message a new client gets right after they pay for [offer]: what happens next, what I need from them, and when. Warm, clear, sets expectations from minute one."

**41. The Deliverable QA** · Claude
"Before I send [deliverable] to [client], check it against the outcome I promised: [paste promise]. List what's missing, what's weak, and what would make it feel premium instead of fine. Be blunt."

**42. Learn From Delivery** · Claude · *(recast)*
"Here are my notes and feedback from delivering [offer]: [paste]. Pull the patterns: what clients value most, where I lose time, and what I should standardize next time. Name the one change with the biggest payoff."

**43. The Testimonial Ask** · Either
"Write the message I send after a good result to get a testimonial for [offer]. Make it effortless: give them 3 specific questions so they don't face a blank page, and offer to draft something from their answers that they can edit."

## STAGE 7 — GROW & RAISE YOUR RATES
*Turn one client into repeat work, proof, and referrals. Seven prompts.*

**44. The Repeat-Client Offer** · Either
"My client just finished [offer]. Write the natural next offer that keeps us working together, and the message that proposes it without being pushy. Tie it directly to the result they just got."

**45. The Case Study** · Claude
"Turn this client win into a short case study: the situation, what I did, the result with a number, and a pull-quote. One that works as proof on my page and in a DM. Use only the real details: [paste]."

**46. The Weekly Review** · Either · *(recast)*
"Here's what I did this week toward my income goal [goal]: [list]. Sort each item into busywork / leverage / unclear — judged only against the goal, not vibes. Name the one highest-leverage thing I underspent on, and the one thing to cut next week. Be blunt."

**47. The Content Engine** · Either · *(recast)*
"Build a 4-week content plan that brings my offer [paste] in front of buyers. 3 posts a week tied to the problems my offer solves; each a specific, postable line in my voice, with the channel and angle. No topic placeholders."

**48. The Win-Back** · Either · *(recast)*
"Write a 3-message win-back to a past client or cold lead for [offer]. Honest and low-pressure, no guilt. Message 1 names the gap; message 2 gives one real reason to come back; message 3 is a clean, friendly door-still-open."

**49. The Referral Engine** · Claude
"Design a simple referral system for my [offer]: who to ask, when to ask, what to offer them, and the exact words. Something I can run after every good result without it feeling transactional."

**50. The Next-Rung Planner** · Either
"My income now is [paste] and I want it at [target]. Map the next 90 days: the one offer to push, the one skill to add, the price to move to, and the weekly action that gets me there. One page, realistic for my hours."
