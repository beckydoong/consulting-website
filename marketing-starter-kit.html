Before the Prompts

First, Gather Your Ammo.

The prompts in this kit run on data. YOUR data. The more specific you are, the more useful the output. Before you start, spend 20 minutes collecting these five things. You probably have most of them already.

1 · Your career history

Pull up your resume or LinkedIn. List your last 3-5 roles, what you actually did (not the job description, the real work), and any results you can quantify. Revenue generated, costs saved, people managed, projects shipped, audiences grown. Numbers matter here.

2 · Your current numbers

Write down your current salary or income, your total compensation (including benefits, bonuses, equity), and your hours worked per week. Be honest. If you're freelancing, include your effective hourly rate after accounting for unbilled time.

3 · What you're good at AND what you enjoy

These are not always the same. Make two lists. The first: skills people come to you for, things that feel easy to you but hard for others, tasks you do faster or better than your peers. The second: work that doesn't drain you, problems you'd solve for free, topics you voluntarily read about.

The overlap between those two lists is where the money is.

4 · Your proof

Collect 5-10 pieces of evidence that you're good at what you do. Performance reviews, client testimonials, Slack messages where someone thanked you, metrics from projects you led, screenshots of results. Women tend to forget this stuff. Don't. It's currency.

5 · What you actually want

Be specific. "More money" isn't specific enough. Do you want a $20K raise at your current job? Do you want to go freelance? Do you want to build a digital product that earns while you sleep? Do you want to leave your industry entirely? The prompts work for all of these, but the output changes depending on where you're headed.

Now you're ready

With these five things in hand, every prompt in this kit will produce output that's specific to YOUR situation. Not generic career advice. Not "10 tips for success." Real analysis, real numbers, real next steps based on where you are right now.

Prompt 01 · Career Intelligence

1.The Hidden Income Audit

The problem

Most women are sitting on skills worth 2-3x what they're currently earning. The gap isn't talent. It's visibility. You don't know what the market would pay for what you already know how to do, because no one has ever mapped it out for you.

You've been so busy executing that you've never stepped back to ask: which of my skills is the most valuable, to whom, and how do I charge for it?

Why it works

This prompt analyzes your career history, daily work, and expertise to identify the specific skills the market pays premium for. It doesn't tell you to "learn to code" or "get a certification." It finds what you already have and shows you who's paying top dollar for it right now.

The prompt

Hidden Income Audit You are a career strategist who specializes in helping women identify and monetize their highest-value skills. I'm going to give you my career data. Analyze it and tell me where the money is.

My career history:
[paste your resume, LinkedIn summary, or a bulleted list of roles + what you actually did in each]

Skills people come to me for: [list 5-10 things people ask you to help with, formally or informally]
What feels easy to me but hard for others: [list 3-5 things]
Current salary/income: [number]
Industry: [your field]

Now give me:

1. My top 3 highest-value skills based on current market demand. For each, tell me what it's worth (salary range for employed roles, hourly/project rate for freelance), who pays the most for it, and why it's more valuable than I probably think.
2. The income gap between what I'm earning and what these skills command in the market. Be specific with numbers.
3. 3 immediate moves I could make in the next 30 days to close that gap (a raise conversation, a freelance gig, a role shift, a side offer).
4. 1 skill combination that's uniquely mine. Most people have one of these skills. What's my specific combination that makes me hard to replace?
Top skill: "Project management + client communication in regulated industries. This combination pays $140-185K in pharma/fintech, vs your current $105K in general tech. The regulated-industry premium alone is worth $30K+."

Immediate move: "Update your LinkedIn headline to include 'regulated industries' and 'cross-functional.' Recruiters in fintech search these terms 4x more than generic PM titles."

Pro tips

  • Be brutally honest about what you do daily. The skills you use without thinking about them are usually the most valuable.
  • Include informal skills. If you're the person who always gets pulled into client calls, that's a monetizable skill.
  • Run this again after every role change. Your skill stack evolves faster than you think.
Prompt 02 · Market Research

2.The Salary Intelligence Report

The problem

You can't negotiate what you don't know. Most women undervalue themselves by 15-30% because they're working off outdated data, gut feelings, or what a coworker mentioned three years ago.

Glassdoor gives you a range so wide it's useless. LinkedIn salary insights are better but still vague. What you need is a research brief that accounts for YOUR specific role, YOUR experience level, YOUR location, and YOUR industry.

Why it works

This prompt builds a compensation research report tailored to your exact situation. It tells you what to expect, what to ask for, and where the leverage points are. Use it before a salary negotiation, a freelance rate conversation, or a job search.

The prompt

Salary Intelligence Report You are a compensation analyst. Build me a detailed salary intelligence report for my specific situation.

My current role: [title + 1-sentence description of what you do]
Years of experience: [total + years in current field]
Location: [city/state, or "remote"]
Industry: [your industry]
Company size: [startup / mid-market / enterprise / freelance]
Current total comp: [salary + bonus + benefits estimate]
Key skills/certifications: [list your top 5]

Give me:

1. Market rate range for my role (25th, 50th, 75th, 90th percentile) with sources cited where possible.
2. Where I fall in that range and why.
3. The 3 highest-paying industries for my skill set, with specific salary ranges for each.
4. Remote vs. on-site premium/discount for my role.
5. Skills that would bump me to the next tier (be specific, not "leadership skills" but "people management of 5+ direct reports" or "SQL proficiency").
6. If I were freelancing this skill set, what hourly and project rates should I charge? What's the realistic monthly income at 25 billable hours/week?
"Your current $92K puts you at the 35th percentile for Senior Marketing Managers in Austin with 8 years experience. The 75th percentile is $128K. The gap: you're missing people-management experience on paper (even though you're doing it informally). Adding '3 direct reports' to your title/scope closes $20K of that gap immediately."

Pro tips

  • Run this before every annual review. Walk in with data, not feelings.
  • Cross-reference with real job postings. After running this prompt, search LinkedIn Jobs for roles matching the top-tier description. Save 5 that list salary ranges.
  • Don't skip the freelance section. Even if you're employed full-time, knowing your freelance rate changes how you think about your time.
Prompt 03 · Strategic Planning

3.The Career Leverage Map

The problem

Working harder doesn't get you paid more. Leverage does. But "build leverage" is vague advice. What does that actually mean for your specific situation? A raise? A role change? A side income stream? A complete pivot?

Most women don't have a plan. They have a feeling that something needs to change and a vague intention to figure it out eventually. Eventually never comes because today's tasks always win.

Why it works

This prompt takes your situation (where you are, what you want, what you have to work with) and maps it to specific moves with timelines. Not generic career advice. A plan with steps, deadlines, and expected outcomes.

The prompt

Career Leverage Map You are a career strategist. I want a concrete 90-day plan to increase my earning power. Not vague advice. Specific moves with timelines and expected outcomes.

Where I am now: [role, salary, industry, company size]
What I want: [be specific: "$130K+ salary" or "replace my income with freelance" or "launch a side business making $3K/mo"]
My constraints: [hours available per week outside work, financial runway, family obligations, etc.]
What I've already tried: [asked for a raise, applied to jobs, started a side project, etc.]
My top skills (from Prompt #1): [paste your top 3]

Give me:

1. The fastest path to my goal given my constraints. Be honest about trade-offs.
2. A 90-day plan broken into 3 phases (30/60/90 days) with specific weekly actions.
3. The one move that has the highest ROI for my specific situation. What should I do FIRST?
4. What to stop doing. Activities that feel productive but aren't moving the needle.
5. The risk assessment. What could go wrong with this plan and how to mitigate it.
"Fastest path: internal promotion + side consulting, not a job search. Here's why: your company just lost two senior PMs. Your manager needs you. That's leverage. Phase 1 (days 1-30): Document your impact in Q1-Q2, schedule the conversation, come with the Salary Intelligence Report. Ask for $118K (15% above current). Phase 2 (days 31-60): While that processes, take on 2 freelance clients at $150/hr using your regulated-industry PM skills. Phase 3: Evaluate. If the raise lands, freelance becomes upside. If it doesn't, you have proof of market rate and 2 paying clients."

Pro tips

  • Be specific about constraints. "I have 10 hours a week" produces a wildly different plan than "I have 3 hours."
  • Include what you've already tried. The AI won't waste your time suggesting things that didn't work.
  • Print the 90-day plan. Put it where you'll see it daily. Review weekly.
Prompt 04 · AI Fluency

4.The AI Skill Stack

The problem

Everyone says "learn AI." No one says which tools, in what order, for your specific career. So you try ChatGPT once, get a mediocre result, and go back to doing everything manually. Meanwhile the person in the next office is quietly using AI to do in 2 hours what takes you 8.

The gap isn't intelligence. It's a learning path that actually fits your work.

Why it works

This prompt builds a personalized 30-day AI learning plan based on YOUR role, YOUR industry, and YOUR daily tasks. It tells you exactly which tools to learn, in what order, with specific exercises using your real work. Not a generic "intro to ChatGPT" course. A plan that makes you measurably faster at your actual job within a month.

The prompt

AI Skill Stack You are an AI productivity coach. Build me a personalized 30-day plan to become proficient with AI tools for my specific role. I don't want a generic tutorial. I want a plan that makes me measurably faster at my actual job.

My role: [title + what you do day-to-day]
My industry: [field]
Top 5 tasks I spend the most time on weekly:
[list them with approximate hours per task]
My current AI experience: ["never used it" / "tried ChatGPT a few times" / "use it occasionally" / "use it daily"]
What I want to get faster at: [specific outcomes: "write reports in half the time" / "automate client updates" / "research competitors" / etc.]

Give me:

1. The 3 AI tools I should learn in priority order, with a 1-sentence explanation of why each one matters for my role.
2. A 30-day plan (Week 1-4) with one specific exercise per day that uses my REAL work as the training material. Not hypothetical examples.
3. The "quick wins": 3 tasks from my list above that I can automate or accelerate THIS WEEK with a single prompt.
4. Time saved estimate. After 30 days, how many hours per week should I expect to reclaim? Be realistic.
5. How to show this to my boss. A 2-sentence talking point I can use to position my AI skills as an asset in my next review.
"Week 1, Day 1: Take that weekly status report you spend 45 minutes on. Paste last week's raw notes into Claude and use this prompt: 'Turn these notes into a concise status update for my manager. Format: 3 accomplishments, 2 blockers, 1 ask. Professional tone, under 200 words.' Edit for 5 minutes. You just saved 40 minutes. That's your baseline."

Pro tips

  • Start with the task you hate most. Motivation is highest when you're automating something painful.
  • Save your best prompts. After 30 days you'll have 15-20 prompts tailored to your job. That library is the real asset.
  • Track time saved. Write it down. You'll need these numbers when you ask for a raise or pitch freelance clients.
Prompt 05 · Business Building

5.The Side Business Validator

The problem

You have an idea for a business. Maybe a coaching practice. Maybe a digital product. Maybe a service based on what you're already good at. But you don't know if it'll actually work. So you either overthink it for six months or build something nobody ends up paying for.

The fix isn't more thinking. It's a structured test that tells you whether this idea has legs before you spend a dollar or quit your job.

Why it works

This prompt runs your business idea through the same validation framework that startup accelerators use: market demand, willingness to pay, competitive landscape, and your unique ability to deliver. It doesn't just say "good idea" or "bad idea." It tells you exactly what to test first, how to test it, and what the numbers need to look like for it to be worth your time.

The prompt

Side Business Validator You are a business strategist who helps women turn expertise into income. I have a business idea and I want you to pressure-test it before I invest time or money.

My idea: [describe in 2-3 sentences: what you'd sell, to whom, at roughly what price]
Why I think I can do this: [your relevant skills/experience]
Time I can invest: [hours per week alongside current job]
Income goal: [how much you want this to make per month within 6 months]
What I've seen in the market: [competitors, similar offers, what's out there]

Give me:

1. Viability score (1-10) with honest reasoning. Don't be polite. Be useful.
2. The math. To hit my income goal, how many customers/clients do I need per month, at what price, with what conversion rate? Is this realistic given my time constraints?
3. The 3 biggest risks and how to mitigate each one.
4. A 2-week validation test. The cheapest, fastest way to find out if real people will pay for this before I build anything.
5. What I should charge. Based on the market and my positioning, give me a specific price (not a range) and explain why.
6. The "instead" option. If this idea scores below a 6, suggest 1-2 alternative business models that better match my skills and constraints.
"Viability: 7/10. The idea (career coaching for women in tech) has real demand, but your positioning is too broad. Narrow to 'negotiation coaching for women in their first management role' and you can charge $500/session instead of $150. The math: 6 clients/month at $500 = $3K. You need 12 discovery calls/month at a 50% close rate. That's 3 calls/week. Doable in 5 hours."

Pro tips

  • Run this on 3 different ideas. Compare the viability scores and math side by side. The winner is usually obvious.
  • Actually do the 2-week validation test. The prompt gives you the test. Don't skip it. Ideas that survive real-world contact are the only ones worth building.
  • Don't optimize the idea. Validate it. The goal isn't to make the prompt output perfect. It's to find out if people will pay.
Prompt 06 · Negotiation

6.The Negotiation Prep Kit

The problem

The negotiation is won before you walk in the room. Most women know this intellectually but still wing it. They walk in with a number that's too low, no data to back it up, and no plan for handling objections. The result: they leave thousands on the table. Every year.

The average woman who negotiates her salary effectively earns $1M+ more over her career than one who doesn't. This isn't about confidence. It's about preparation.

Why it works

This prompt builds your complete negotiation playbook: the number to ask for, the data that supports it, the exact words to use, and scripts for every objection your manager is likely to raise. It works for salary negotiations, freelance rate conversations, scope discussions, and new job offers.

The prompt

Negotiation Prep Kit You are a negotiation coach who specializes in compensation conversations. Help me prepare for an upcoming negotiation so I walk in with a case, not just a request.

What I'm negotiating: [raise at current job / new job offer / freelance rate / project scope]
My current comp: [salary/rate]
What I want: [specific number or range]
My market data (from Prompt #2): [paste relevant findings]
My top 3 accomplishments this year: [with numbers where possible]
Who I'm negotiating with: [their role, what they care about, your relationship]
My biggest fear about this conversation: [be honest]

Give me:

1. The opening statement. Exact words I should use to start the conversation. Under 60 seconds when spoken aloud.
2. The evidence package. My 3 strongest proof points, structured as: "I did [X], which resulted in [Y], and this is worth [Z] to the company."
3. Objection scripts for the 5 most likely pushbacks (budget constraints, timing, "we'll revisit in 6 months," peer equity, etc.) with word-for-word responses.
4. The silence strategy. When to stop talking and let them respond.
5. The walk-away line. What to say if they say no, that keeps the door open without accepting a bad deal.
6. The follow-up email. A draft email to send within 24 hours that documents what was agreed (or what was discussed if no agreement yet).
Opening: "I wanted to talk about my compensation. In the last year, I've [specific achievement]. Based on market data for this role in our industry, I'm currently below the 50th percentile. I'd like to discuss moving to [specific number], which reflects both my contributions and the market."

Objection script for "We don't have the budget right now": "I understand budget timing. Can we agree on the number and set a specific date for implementation? I'd also be open to discussing a one-time bonus to bridge the gap, or additional PTO as part of the package."

Pro tips

  • Practice the opening out loud 5 times. Reading it silently is not the same as saying it.
  • Bring a printed one-pager. When you slide data across the table, it signals you've done your homework.
  • Never give the first number? Wrong. Anchoring works. State your number first, make it specific (not rounded), and back it with data.
Prompt 07 · Visibility

7.The Personal Brand Blueprint

The problem

The raise, the clients, the partnerships, the speaking invitations, the "how did you hear about me?" inbound leads. They all go to the woman who's visible. Not the one who's best. Visibility is leverage, and most women aren't building it because "personal branding" feels performative and vague.

It doesn't have to be. A personal brand is just a system for making sure the right people know what you're good at. That's it.

Why it works

This prompt creates a concrete 4-week plan to become visible in your field. Not "post more on LinkedIn." A specific strategy based on your expertise, your audience, and the opportunities you want to attract. At the end of 4 weeks, you'll have a body of content, a growing network, and a clear identity in your space.

The prompt

Personal Brand Blueprint You are a personal brand strategist. Build me a 4-week plan to establish visibility in my field. I don't want to be an influencer. I want the right people to know what I'm good at so opportunities come to me instead of me chasing them.

My expertise: [what you're known for or want to be known for]
My target audience: [who should see your content: hiring managers, potential clients, industry peers, etc.]
Opportunities I want to attract: [job offers, freelance clients, speaking invitations, partnerships, etc.]
Primary platform: [LinkedIn / Instagram / X / etc.]
Time I can invest: [hours per week]
My comfort level: ["I've never posted publicly" / "I post occasionally" / "I post regularly but without strategy"]

Give me:

1. My positioning statement. One sentence that describes what I do and who it's for. Something I'd put in my LinkedIn headline.
2. 5 content pillars. The recurring topics I should own. For each, give me 3 specific post ideas.
3. A 4-week posting calendar. Exactly what to post, when, in what format (text, carousel, video, comment strategy). 3-5 posts per week.
4. The "warm outreach" plan. 5 people I should connect with this month and exactly what to say to each (not generic networking, strategic relationship building).
5. The metric that matters. What should I track to know this is working? (Hint: it's not follower count.)
6. Week 1 quick start. The single post I should publish tomorrow to get started, fully written and ready to copy-paste.
Positioning: "I help SaaS companies reduce churn through customer success systems. 8 years in CS leadership, $4M in retained revenue last year."

Content pillar #1: "Churn autopsy" — break down a real (anonymized) churn situation and what I'd do differently.
Post idea: "We lost a $180K account last quarter. Here's the email I should have sent 6 weeks earlier."

Week 1 quick start: "I've spent 8 years in customer success. Here are 3 things I wish someone had told me in year one: [list]. Which one resonates? Tell me in the comments."

Pro tips

  • Consistency beats quality for the first month. A decent post published beats a perfect post in your drafts.
  • Comment on 5 posts from people in your field every day. This builds visibility faster than your own posts.
  • Repurpose everything. A good LinkedIn post becomes an email, an Instagram carousel, and a talking point in your next meeting.
